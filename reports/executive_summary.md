## 1. Executive Overview

Retail customer attrition is actively eroding our European banking balance sheet. Across the analyzed portfolio of 10,000 customer accounts, the institution experiences a baseline churn rate of **20.37%** (2,037 defected accounts). 

Crucially, customer attrition is not distributed evenly across transactional, low-balance accounts. Rather, it is concentrated in our most affluent and mature customer cohorts. This dynamic has resulted in a cumulative **Balance Exposure of €185.59 million** in core liquid deposits drained from the institution.

## 2. Four Structural Risk Drivers

### 1. Geographic Capital Flight in Germany (Risk Index: 1.59)
- **Observed Churn:** **32.44%** in Germany versus **16.15%** in France and **16.67%** in Spain.
- **Capital Exposure:** German accounts represent **€102.41 million** (55.2%) of all lost balance.
- **Root Cause:** Saturated German market with aggressive fee-free offerings from cooperative banks (*Volksbanken*) and savings banks (*Sparkassen*), coupled with high customer sensitivity to account maintenance fees.

### 2. The 46–60 Age Cohort Crisis (56.21% Churn)
- Customers aged 46 to 60 exhibit an alarming **56.21% defection rate** (1,154 churned accounts out of 2,053).
- In contrast, customers under 30 churn at just **7.52%**.
- **Root Cause:** This demographic represents peak earning and pre-retirement wealth restructuring (mortgage payoff, wealth transfer, university financing). Transactional retail branches fail to provide specialized advisory services, driving these high-balance clients to private banking boutiques.

### 3. The Multi-Product Hazard Curve
- **The Golden Anchor:** Customers with **2 products** demonstrate the lowest churn across the institution at **7.58%**.
- **The Cross-Selling Hazard:** Churn spikes to **82.71%** for customers with 3 products, and reaches **100.00%** (all 60 accounts) for customers with 4 products.
- **Root Cause:** Aggressive cross-selling quotas pushed redundant ancillary products (store cards, complex insurance riders) onto customers without genuine utility, creating billing friction and relationship fatigue.

### 4. The Wealth Paradox: High-Value Concentration
- Churning customers hold an average balance of **€91,108**, compared to **€72,745** for retained accounts (+25.2% higher).
- In the High-Value segment ($Balance > €50k \land Salary > €100k$), **691 accounts defected (28.67% churn)**, taking **€83.38 million** with them—representing nearly **45% of all lost capital**.
